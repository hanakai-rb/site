## Q&A with Carolyn Cole

<img src="/blog/assets/2026-09-02-thanking-our-silver-sponsors-and-carolyn-cole/carolyn.webp" alt="Carolyn Cole" title="Carolyn Cole" class="float-image float-image--end">

This week we're lucky to have [Carolyn Cole](https://github.com/carolyncole) join us for a Q&A!

Carolyn works at [Princeton University Libraries](https://library.princeton.edu) and is our community's resident expert at porting Rails apps to Hanami. She gave a workshop on this topic at this year's RubyConf, and her team's [orcid_princeton_hanami](https://github.com/pulibrary/orcid_princeton_hanami) serves as a fantastic open source example of a real world Hanami app.

We continue to see great things from from Carolyn and her team, including this overview of [porting a Rails app to plain Rack alongside Dry Monads and Rom](https://pulibrary.github.io/2026-08-03-non-rails-rack). If any of this is your interest, be sure to [follow their work](https://github.com/pulibrary)! In the meantime, let's hear from Carolyn herself.

**Who are you, and what are you building?**

I’m Carolyn Cole. I work at the [Princeton University Libraries](https://library.princeton.edu). I have been building software for the past 30 years. Libraries in general have become the stewards of information both physical and digital. My team focuses on preserving and making born digital data created by the researchers at Princeton University widely available.

**What made you reach for Hanakai, and what does your stack look like today?**

I have wanted to utilize functional Ruby, in the form of [Dry](https://hanakai.org/dry) ever since I saw a presentation at RubyConf (I cannot remember the year). However, I could not get any interest from team members at the time. After attending a workshop at RubyConf Chicago I was re-energized to try Hanami. I lobbied again to use Hanami and got the go ahead to convert one of our Rails applications into Hanami

**What's a moment building with Hanakai that made you think, "yes, this is why"?**

The organization that Hanami provides to keep your logic out of your templates and actions made my new Hanami application easier to read than the existing Rails application. As we all know reading your code is a larger cost than creating it, so having more organization is a definite benefit.

**What are you excited about for the future?**

I am hoping to convert some more of our applications over to Hanami. We have a wizard controller that is over 200 lines long. I can’t wait to break that controller up into more meaningful pieces.
